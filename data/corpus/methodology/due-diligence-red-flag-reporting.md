## Overview

A red flag report is a short, fast-turnaround escalation of any due diligence finding material enough to affect the deal decision — price, structure, or whether to proceed at all. It exists precisely because a comprehensive final diligence report, however thorough, often lands too late in the deal timeline for its findings to actually change the outcome.

## When to Use It

Use continuously throughout any due diligence workstream (commercial or financial), from day one of the engagement through signing — not as a single deliverable at the end. Any finding that clears the materiality bar gets escalated as soon as it's identified.

## Framework

1. **Set the materiality bar upfront with the deal team.** Agree early what threshold of financial or strategic impact warrants a red flag escalation, so the diligence team isn't making that judgment call ad hoc under time pressure partway through the engagement.
2. **Escalate immediately, not in the final report.** A red flag report is typically a one-page summary sent as soon as a material finding is confirmed — deal timelines move fast enough that a finding held for the final comprehensive report may arrive after the point where it could have changed price or structure.
3. **Distinguish confirmed findings from open questions.** A red flag report should clearly separate what's been verified from what still needs follow-up — escalating unconfirmed suspicions with the same urgency as verified findings erodes the deal team's trust in the signal.
4. **Recommend, don't just report.** A strong red flag report includes a view on materiality and possible mitigations (price adjustment, specific indemnity, structural change, or walk-away) — not just a neutral statement of fact for the deal team to interpret unaided.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Batching findings for the final report.** The single most common failure mode — it defeats the purpose of red flag reporting, which exists specifically to get material findings to decision-makers while there's still time to act.
- **No agreed materiality threshold.** Without a pre-agreed bar, individual team members apply inconsistent judgment about what warrants escalation, leading to either alarm fatigue (too many low-materiality flags) or missed findings (too conservative a bar).
- **Reporting without a recommendation.** A red flag report that states a fact without a view on what it means for the deal leaves the deal team to do interpretive work under time pressure that the diligence team is better placed to do.

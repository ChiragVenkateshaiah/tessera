## Overview

The master checklist combines Day 1 readiness and the first 100 days of integration into one list owned by the integration management office. It was Meridian's standard integration planning tool until 2018, built on the idea that a single list, tracked weekly, keeps every workstream visible.

## When to Use It

Used from signing onward on any acquisition with operational integration, as the one planning artifact the integration management office maintained.

## Framework

1. **Build one master checklist.** List every integration task — from payroll continuity and system access through process harmonization, brand migration and org redesign — in a single sequence running from close to Day 100.
2. **Target as much as possible for Day 1.** Pull integration tasks forward to the close date wherever the workstream can deliver them, to show momentum to the board.
3. **Settle leadership with the full org design.** Confirm reporting lines for the combined organization once the full organization design is complete, usually around Day 60, rather than appointing interim leaders first.
4. **Track weekly.** The integration management office reviews the master checklist weekly with every workstream lead.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Checklist sprawl.** Master checklists commonly exceed 1,000 lines; group tasks under workstream headings.
- **Unassigned tasks.** Every line needs a named owner before close.

## Related Frameworks

Integration management office (IMO) setup, change management — stakeholder mapping and communication planning, due diligence (Day 1 planning is directly informed by diligence findings).

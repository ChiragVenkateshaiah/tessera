## Overview

A communication plan sequences and tailors what gets communicated to whom, through what channel, at what point in a change program — turning the stakeholder map into an actual engagement cadence. Poorly sequenced communication (too early, too late, or too generic) is one of the most common ways well-designed change programs lose organizational support.

## When to Use It

Use once stakeholder mapping is complete, as the direct next step in any change management workstream. Applies across reorganizations, cost programs, system rollouts, and integration programs.

## Framework

1. **Sequence by audience readiness, not by convenience.** High-influence stakeholders and those most directly affected typically need to hear about a change before a broad announcement — finding out through a company-wide email rather than a direct conversation is a fast way to convert a neutral stakeholder into a resistant one.
2. **Tailor message and channel to audience.** Senior leadership needs strategic rationale and their specific role in cascading the message; frontline employees typically need concrete, practical detail about what changes for them personally, delivered through a channel and by a messenger (often their direct manager, not a company-wide broadcast) they trust.
3. **Build two-way channels, not just broadcast.** A communication plan that only pushes information out misses the chance to surface concerns early, when they're still addressable, and to detect where the stakeholder map's resistance assumptions were wrong.
4. **Plan the silence, not just the announcements.** Gaps in communication during a change program get filled by rumor. A deliberate cadence of regular updates — even "no new decision yet" updates — prevents an information vacuum from becoming a trust problem.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Broad announcement before targeted engagement.** Announcing a change broadly before the highest-influence, most-affected stakeholders have been engaged directly damages trust with exactly the people whose support matters most.
- **One-size-fits-all messaging.** A single message and channel used across audiences with very different concerns and information needs under-serves most of them.
- **Communication silence during implementation.** Once the initial announcement is made, programs often go quiet during the harder implementation phase — exactly when rumor and anxiety are most likely to fill the gap.

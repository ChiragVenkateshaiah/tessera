## Overview

Roadmap sequencing takes a set of desired digital capabilities and initiatives — usually more than a client can fund or execute simultaneously — and orders them into a realistic, phased plan. The sequencing logic matters as much as the initiative list itself: the same set of initiatives executed in a different order can succeed or fail depending on whether foundational dependencies are respected.

## When to Use It

Use after the technology capability assessment has established the current-state baseline and binding constraints, as the direct next step in any digital transformation engagement.

## Framework

1. **Separate foundational from customer-facing initiatives.** Foundational work (data infrastructure, core system modernization, cloud migration) often doesn't generate visible client-facing value on its own but unlocks initiatives that do. A roadmap that leads entirely with customer-facing initiatives before foundational work is complete tends to produce fragile, hard-to-scale early wins.
2. **Sequence around the binding constraint identified in the capability assessment.** If a legacy core system gates most future digital initiatives, either address it early (even though it's slow, unglamorous work) or explicitly design around it — building further initiatives that depend on it without addressing the constraint compounds technical debt.
3. **Balance quick wins against foundational investment.** A roadmap of pure foundational work with no visible near-term value struggles to sustain organizational and budget support over a multi-year horizon. Interleave early, lower-risk initiatives that demonstrate value while foundational work proceeds in parallel.
4. **Build in explicit decision points, not just a static plan.** Digital transformation roadmaps span multiple years, over which both technology and competitive context shift. Structure the roadmap with defined checkpoints where scope and sequencing get deliberately reassessed, rather than presenting a multi-year plan as fixed at the outset.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Leading with customer-facing work over foundational dependencies.** Produces early, visible wins that are expensive to scale or maintain because the underlying foundation wasn't ready.
- **All foundational, no visible value.** Loses organizational sponsorship over a multi-year horizon if there's nothing tangible to show before the foundational phase completes.
- **Treating the roadmap as fixed.** A multi-year plan presented without built-in reassessment points becomes stale and is often followed past the point where it still makes sense, simply because changing it feels disruptive.

## Related Frameworks

Technology capability assessment (direct predecessor), operating model design — roadmap sequencing frequently has organizational dependencies alongside technical ones.

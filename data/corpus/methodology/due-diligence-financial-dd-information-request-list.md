## Overview

This is the standing information request list the financial diligence workstream issues into the data room. It is a working checklist, not a narrative page — the reasoning behind each area sits in the financial due diligence methodology. Copy this list, delete what doesn't apply to the deal, and add sector-specific items before issuing.

## How to Use It

Issue in two waves. Wave one is everything needed to build the quality-of-earnings bridge and the net debt schedule; wave two is everything triggered by what wave one reveals. Issuing all fifty-odd items at once buries the target's finance team and reliably produces slow, low-quality responses on the items that actually matter.

Mark each item as received, partially received, or outstanding at every workstream stand-up. An item that has been "partially received" for more than a week is an escalation, not an administrative status.

## Request List

### Revenue and Contracts

- [ ] Revenue by product, customer, and geography, monthly, for the last three full years and the current year to date
- [ ] Revenue recognition policy, and a note on any change in policy or estimate over the period
- [ ] Top twenty customers by revenue, with contract start and end dates
  - [ ] Change-of-control, assignment, and termination-for-convenience clauses for each
  - [ ] Renewal history and any customer that has served notice
- [ ] Pricing history by major product line, including discount and rebate schedules
- [ ] Deferred revenue and contract liability balances, with ageing
- [ ] Backlog and pipeline definitions, with the conversion rates historically achieved against each

### Cost and Margin

- [ ] Gross margin by product line, monthly, for the last three full years
- [ ] Cost of sales build, separating direct labour, materials, and allocated overhead
- [ ] Headcount by function and location, month-end, for the last three years
  - [ ] Any restructuring or redundancy programme run in the period, with cost and expected run-rate benefit
- [ ] Full general ledger trial balance extract for each period presented
- [ ] Management adjustments already reflected in the reported EBITDA figure, with supporting evidence for each
- [ ] One-off, non-recurring, and exceptional items schedule, with the basis for classifying each as non-recurring

### Working Capital

- [ ] Monthly working capital balances (receivables, inventory, payables) for at least twenty-four months
- [ ] Debtor ageing and bad debt provision movement
- [ ] Inventory ageing, obsolescence provision, and any write-off history
- [ ] Creditor ageing, and evidence of any supplier payment terms extended in the last eighteen months
- [ ] Cash conversion cycle by month, with commentary on seasonality
- [ ] Any factoring, supply chain finance, or receivables discounting arrangement in place

### Debt and Debt-Like Items

- [ ] Full debt schedule with facility agreements, covenants, and maturity profile
- [ ] Pension scheme valuation and funding position, including any deficit recovery plan
- [ ] Deferred and contingent consideration from prior acquisitions
- [ ] Lease schedule, separating property, vehicle, and equipment leases
- [ ] Onerous or loss-making contract provisions
- [ ] Related-party balances and loans, in both directions
- [ ] Any guarantee, indemnity, or letter of comfort given to a third party

### Capital Expenditure

- [ ] Capex by category, split maintenance versus growth, for the last three years
- [ ] Fixed asset register with age profile and depreciation policy
- [ ] Committed but unspent capex, and any capital project mid-delivery
- [ ] Management's own assessment of deferred maintenance capex, if one exists

### Tax

- [ ] Corporate tax computations and returns for all open years
- [ ] Status of any open enquiry, audit, or assessment in any jurisdiction
- [ ] Transfer pricing documentation and intra-group pricing policy
- [ ] Indirect tax (VAT/GST/sales tax) position and any registration gaps
- [ ] Deferred tax asset recognition basis, including loss carry-forwards

### Commitments and Contingencies

- [ ] Litigation and claims register, with legal counsel's assessment of each
- [ ] Regulatory correspondence and any open regulatory matter
- [ ] Insurance policies, claims history, and any uninsured exposure
- [ ] Off-balance-sheet arrangements of any kind
- [ ] Environmental liabilities or site remediation obligations

### Systems and Controls

- [ ] Chart of accounts and finance system landscape
- [ ] Auditor management letters and internal control findings for the last three years
- [ ] Description of the month-end close process and its typical duration
- [ ] Any restatement, prior-period adjustment, or audit qualification in the period

## Common Pitfalls

- **Issuing the full list on day one.** It signals inexperience, overwhelms a finance team that is usually running diligence alongside its day job, and delays the wave-one items the quality-of-earnings bridge actually depends on.
- **Accepting a summary where the underlying data was requested.** A management-prepared summary of one-off items is a starting point for a conversation, not evidence. Ask for what sits beneath it.
- **Treating an unanswered item as a scheduling problem.** Items that stay outstanding through to the end of diligence are findings in their own right and belong in the red flag report, not in an appendix of outstanding requests.

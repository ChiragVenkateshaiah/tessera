## Overview

Competitive landscape mapping identifies who else is fighting for the opportunity a client is evaluating, and how strong they are. It's the stage of market entry analysis most likely to change a client's mind — a market that looks attractive on a sizing slide often looks very different once you map who already owns the incumbent relationships.

## When to Use It

As stage 3 of the market entry framework, after sizing, before the entry-mode recommendation. It can also stand alone when a client already knows the market is big enough and just wants a competitive read.

## Framework

**Build the competitor set in three tiers.**
- *Direct competitors* — same product, same segment, same geography.
- *Adjacent competitors* — could plausibly move into this space (e.g., a player strong in a neighboring segment or geography).
- *Substitute threats* — different product form that solves the same underlying customer need.

**Score each competitor on a small number of dimensions**, typically: market share/scale, relative cost position, brand strength, distribution reach, and pace of innovation. Resist the urge to score on twelve dimensions — a 2x2 or simple radar chart on the 3-4 dimensions that actually differentiate players in this market is more useful than an exhaustive matrix nobody will read.

**Map white space.** Once competitors are scored, look for segments or need-states that are underserved — pockets where no competitor scores well. This is usually where a credible entry angle lives, rather than head-on competition against the strongest incumbent.

**Assess likely competitive response.** If the client enters, how will incumbents react — price down, increase marketing spend, accelerate a product roadmap? A market that looks open can close quickly if incumbents are well-capitalized and motivated to defend share.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Only mapping direct competitors.** The substitute-threat tier is the one teams skip most often, and it's frequently where the real disruption risk (or entry opportunity) sits.
- **Static snapshots.** A competitive map is a moment in time. Note which players are gaining or losing share, not just their current position — trajectory matters more than a single data point.
- **No response scenario.** Failing to war-game the incumbent reaction leaves the client's entry-mode recommendation vulnerable to a competitive response nobody anticipated.

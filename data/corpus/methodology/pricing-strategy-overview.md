## Overview

Pricing is the fastest lever a company has to move margin — a 1% price improvement typically drops through to operating profit at a far higher rate than a 1% cost reduction or volume increase, because most of the cost base doesn't move with a price change. Despite this, pricing is often under-invested in relative to cost and growth initiatives, largely because it feels riskier (visible to customers immediately) even though the analytical rigor required is comparable.

## When to Use It

Use this framework as the entry point whenever margin improvement, a competitive pricing threat, or a new product launch raises a pricing question. It links to three companion methodologies: value-based pricing, price elasticity analysis, and competitive pricing response.

## Framework

1. **Diagnose the current pricing approach.** Most companies price using one of three logics, often inconsistently across their portfolio: cost-plus (price = cost + margin target), competitive (price = match or undercut competitor), or value-based (price = a share of the quantified value delivered to the customer). Establish which logic is actually driving current prices before proposing a change.
2. **Segment.** Pricing strategy rarely applies uniformly across a customer base or product line. Segment by willingness-to-pay driver (use case, customer size, competitive intensity in that segment) before setting or changing price.
3. **Select the target logic per segment.** Value-based pricing captures the most margin where a differentiated value proposition exists and can be quantified; competitive pricing is appropriate in commoditized segments; cost-plus is a fallback, not a strategy.
4. **Test before full rollout.** Price changes are highly visible and hard to reverse without reputational cost. Use controlled tests (regional pilots, segment-limited launches) before a full rollout wherever the client's go-to-market model allows it.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Cost-plus pricing by default.** The most common failure mode — pricing that's anchored to internal cost structure rather than customer-perceived value systematically leaves margin on the table in differentiated segments.
- **Uniform pricing across a segmented customer base.** Treating all customers as equally price-sensitive under-prices the segments that would pay more and over-prices the segments that will walk.
- **No test-and-learn discipline.** Full-scale price changes without a pilot phase create unnecessary revenue risk, especially in B2C or highly competitive B2B categories.

## Related Frameworks

Value-based pricing, price elasticity analysis, competitive pricing response, market entry analysis — market sizing (revenue-per-customer assumptions depend on pricing strategy).

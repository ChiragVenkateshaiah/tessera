## Overview

The RACI and governance methodology page explains why decision rights matter and how to run the mapping exercise. This page holds the worked matrix — a starting template for a multi-business-unit organisation, to be adapted rather than adopted. Its value is as a straw model to react to: clients argue productively with a filled-in grid and unproductively with a blank one.

## When to Use It

Use in the design phase of an operating model engagement, once the structure and process layers are drafted and before governance forums are designed. Bring the filled template to a working session, let the client's leadership team mark it up, and treat every disagreement about a cell as a finding about how the organisation actually works today.

## Framework

**The matrix.** R is responsible for doing the work, A is the single accountable decision-maker, C is consulted before the decision, I is informed after it. Rows are decision types; columns are roles. Adapt the role set to the client's actual structure.

| Decision | Group CEO | BU Leader | Group CFO | Group Function Head | BU Function Lead | Board |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Capex above group threshold | C | R | C | I | I | A |
| Capex below group threshold | I | A | C | I | R | — |
| Annual budget approval | A | R | R | C | C | C |
| In-year reforecast | C | A | R | I | R | I |
| Pricing framework and policy | C | C | C | A | R | I |
| Individual customer price exception | I | A | C | C | R | — |
| Hiring above senior grade | A | R | I | C | I | I |
| Hiring below senior grade | — | I | — | I | A | — |
| Strategic vendor selection | C | C | C | A | R | I |
| Local vendor selection | — | I | — | C | A | — |
| Enterprise architecture standards | I | C | I | A | R | — |
| Exception to architecture standard | I | R | I | A | C | — |
| New market or segment entry | R | R | C | C | I | A |
| Brand and identity changes | A | C | I | R | I | I |

**Reading the matrix.** Three tests apply to every row. First, exactly one A — see below. Second, the A must have genuine authority over the resources the decision commits; an accountable party who cannot direct the budget is a fiction. Third, the C list must be short enough to be real. A row with five consulted parties describes a decision that will not get made in the time available, and in practice will be made informally by someone who then seeks retrospective agreement.

**The single-A rule, and the honest exception.** Every row must have exactly one A. Two accountable parties means no accountable party, and the failure mode is not deadlock but silent divergence — each party assumes the other is carrying it. When a client insists a decision is genuinely jointly owned, the productive move is not to allow two As but to decompose the decision until each component has one. "Pricing" split into "framework" and "individual exception" is the common example, and it appears twice in the matrix above for exactly that reason. Where decomposition genuinely fails — usually in true joint ventures with contractual co-approval — record the joint approval as a governance mechanism with a named tie-break, not as two As.

**Thresholds carry as much weight as the grid.** Rows that split on a threshold (capex, hiring grade, vendor spend) are only as useful as the threshold. Set it where it puts roughly the volume of decisions at each level that the people at that level can actually process, and revisit it annually — an unchanged threshold in a growing organisation quietly recentralises authority every year.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Filling the grid in a room without the decision-makers.** A matrix agreed by the programme team and presented to leadership as finished will be politely accepted and then ignored.
- **Confusing the current state with the target state.** Run the exercise twice, explicitly labelled. The gap between the two is the change agenda; a single blended matrix hides it.
- **Leaving thresholds unspecified.** "Significant capex" is not a threshold, and every row that depends on one will be interpreted differently by each business unit.
- **Treating the matrix as the deliverable.** It is an input to governance forum design. If no forum changes as a result, the exercise has not landed.

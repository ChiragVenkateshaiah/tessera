## Overview

RACI (Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed) is the standard technique for making decision rights explicit — for a given decision or activity, who does the work, who is ultimately accountable, who must be consulted before a decision, and who simply needs to be kept informed after. Governance design extends this to the forums (committees, review cadences, escalation paths) through which decisions actually get made and tracked.

## When to Use It

Use RACI mapping whenever an operating model diagnostic surfaces ambiguous accountability as a root cause of slow decisions or duplicated effort — which is most operating model engagements. It's also useful as a lightweight standalone tool when a client just wants clarity on a specific cross-functional process without a full operating model redesign.

## Framework

1. **Select the decisions/activities to map.** Don't attempt to RACI every activity in the organization — focus on the decisions and processes where ambiguity is actually causing friction, typically identified during the operating model diagnostic interviews.
2. **Assign exactly one "Accountable" per row.** The most common RACI failure is assigning multiple people as Accountable for the same decision, which reproduces the exact ambiguity the tool exists to eliminate. Responsible can have multiple parties; Accountable cannot.
3. **Keep Consulted and Informed lists disciplined.** Over-listing people as "Consulted" slows decisions down by requiring broad sign-off; the temptation to be inclusive works against the goal of faster, clearer decisions. Push back on stakeholders who want to be Consulted on everything.
4. **Design the governance cadence around the RACI, not separately.** Once decision rights are mapped, design the actual forums (which committee meets how often, with what decision authority, escalating to whom) so the governance structure enforces the RACI in practice rather than existing as a parallel, disconnected structure.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Multiple "Accountable" owners.** The single most common way RACI mapping fails to change behavior — if two people are both Accountable, nothing has actually changed from an ambiguous status quo.
- **RACI as a one-time document.** A RACI matrix that's built once during the engagement and never referenced again in actual operating rhythm is a slide, not a governance tool — it needs to be embedded into the actual meeting cadence and decision forums.
- **Over-consulting.** Excessive "Consulted" designations recreate the slow, consensus-seeking decision process the RACI exercise was meant to fix.

## Overview

Financial due diligence validates that a target's reported financial performance is real, sustainable, and correctly understood — quality of earnings, working capital normalization, and identification of one-off items that inflate reported profitability. It answers "are the numbers real," distinct from commercial due diligence's "is this a good business."

## When to Use It

Every acquisition process, run in parallel with commercial due diligence, typically by a dedicated financial diligence workstream (often involving specialist accounting resources alongside the core deal team).

## Framework

**Quality of earnings (QoE) analysis.** Reported EBITDA is adjusted for one-off items, related-party transactions not at arm's length, and accounting policy choices that inflate reported profitability, to arrive at a normalized, sustainable earnings figure. This adjusted number — not the reported number — is what should anchor valuation.

**Working capital normalization.** Establish a normalized working capital level (accounting for seasonality) to set the working capital target in the purchase agreement — getting this wrong creates a direct, quantifiable value transfer at close that's easy to miss under deal-timeline pressure.

**Net debt and debt-like items.** Identify all debt and debt-like obligations (including items not obviously labeled as debt — underfunded pension obligations, deferred consideration from prior acquisitions, onerous lease commitments) that need to be deducted from enterprise value to arrive at equity value.

**Checklist of standard review areas:**
- Revenue recognition policy and any recent changes
- Customer concentration and contract terms (particularly any change-of-control clauses)
- Related-party transactions
- Capex history versus maintenance capex requirement (is reported EBITDA overstated because capex has been deferred)
- Tax position and any open tax exposures
- Off-balance-sheet commitments and contingent liabilities

## Common Pitfalls

- **Anchoring on reported EBITDA without normalization.** Skipping QoE adjustment and valuing off headline reported EBITDA is the most common and costly financial diligence failure.
- **Underestimating working capital seasonality.** A working capital target set from a single point-in-time snapshot, without understanding the seasonal cycle, misstates the true normalized level.
- **Missing debt-like items that aren't labeled "debt."** Deferred consideration, underfunded pensions, and onerous contracts are easy to miss in a standard net-debt schedule if the review isn't deliberately looking for them.

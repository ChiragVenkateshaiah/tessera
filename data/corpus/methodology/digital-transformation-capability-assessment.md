## Overview

A technology capability assessment establishes a client's current-state technology maturity — architecture, infrastructure, talent, and delivery practices — as the factual baseline that any digital transformation roadmap has to build from. Skipping this step (jumping straight to a roadmap of desired future capabilities) produces plans that are disconnected from what's actually achievable given the starting point.

## When to Use It

Use at the start of any digital transformation engagement, before roadmap sequencing — the roadmap methodology explicitly depends on this assessment's output.

## Framework

1. **Assess across four dimensions**: architecture (how modular/coupled are core systems — a critical factor in financial services clients running legacy core banking platforms), infrastructure (cloud maturity, scalability), talent and delivery practices (engineering capability, agile/DevOps maturity), and data (quality, accessibility, governance).
2. **Benchmark against realistic peers, not aspirational ones.** Comparing a client's capability against a digital-native competitor sets an unrealistic bar; compare against a peer set with a comparable starting point (similar legacy footprint, similar scale) to identify a credible improvement trajectory.
3. **Identify the binding constraint.** Most organizations have one or two capability gaps that gate everything else — frequently, for financial services clients, a monolithic core banking platform that makes any new digital product slow and expensive to build and ship. Identifying this constraint explicitly is more valuable than a comprehensive but undifferentiated maturity score across all dimensions.
4. **Translate capability gaps into roadmap inputs.** The assessment's output should be structured specifically to feed the roadmap sequencing methodology — capability gaps ranked by how much they gate future initiatives, not just by abstract maturity score.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Aspirational rather than realistic benchmarking.** Benchmarking against digital-native peers produces a capability gap so large it demotivates rather than informs — pick a benchmark set the client can genuinely aspire to close the gap against within a realistic multi-year horizon.
- **Treating all capability gaps as equally important.** A comprehensive maturity assessment without a clear view of which gap is the binding constraint doesn't help prioritize where transformation investment should go first.
- **Assessment as a one-time exercise.** Technology capability shifts meaningfully within 12-18 months of active transformation investment — reassess periodically rather than treating the initial baseline as static.

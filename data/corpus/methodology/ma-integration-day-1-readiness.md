## Overview

Day 1 readiness planning covers everything that must be true the moment a deal legally closes — from the mechanical (payroll continuity, IT access, customer-facing communications) to the substantive (interim leadership and reporting lines) — so the combined organization can operate without disruption from the first day of common ownership, even though full integration will take much longer.

## When to Use It

Use in the period between signing and closing, in parallel with (and informed by) due diligence findings, for any acquisition where the acquirer intends operational integration (as opposed to a hold-separate or minority-investment structure).

## Framework

1. **Build the Day 1 critical path.** Identify everything that must function correctly on the close date itself — payroll, benefits continuity, IT and system access, customer and supplier communications, regulatory notifications — and treat this list as non-negotiable, distinct from the broader integration plan that unfolds over subsequent months.
2. **Assign interim leadership and reporting lines before close.** Employees need to know who they report to and how decisions get made from day one — ambiguity here in the first weeks post-close is a leading driver of unwanted attrition, particularly among the target's most mobile talent.
3. **Prepare Day 1 communications for every audience** — employees, customers, suppliers, and where relevant regulators — coordinated in timing and message with the stakeholder mapping and communication planning methodologies.
4. **Distinguish Day 1 must-haves from Day 100 nice-to-haves.** A common failure is over-scoping the Day 1 plan to include integration work that could reasonably wait — this creates unnecessary risk and pressure in the highest-scrutiny period of the deal, when the organization can least afford execution stumbles.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Under-planning the mechanical basics.** Payroll or system access failures on Day 1 are highly visible, easily avoidable with proper planning, and disproportionately damage confidence in the deal relative to their actual strategic importance.
- **Leadership ambiguity in the critical early weeks.** Delaying interim leadership decisions until "after we've figured out the full org design" leaves a leadership vacuum precisely when clarity matters most for retention.
- **Over-scoping Day 1.** Trying to complete too much substantive integration work by the close date, rather than sequencing it into the following weeks and months, increases execution risk without a corresponding benefit.

## Related Frameworks

## Overview

Procurement cost reduction targets the client's third-party spend — raw materials, indirect purchases, and services contracts — through a combination of demand management, sourcing strategy, and supplier negotiation. For manufacturing and industrials clients in particular, procurement is frequently the single largest addressable cost pool, larger than SG&A.

## When to Use It

Use this as a companion lever within a broader cost transformation program, or standalone when a client specifically flags procurement spend (rather than headcount or overhead) as the primary target.

## Framework

1. **Spend cube analysis.** Categorize all third-party spend by category, supplier, and business unit. This sounds mechanical but is frequently the hardest step — most clients don't have clean, consolidated spend data, and reconciling it across ERP systems and business units is real work.
2. **Segment by addressability.** Not all spend is equally negotiable. Segment into: sourcing-event candidates (competitive categories, multiple viable suppliers), negotiation candidates (concentrated supplier relationships, harder to re-source quickly), and demand-management candidates (spend that's better reduced by consuming less, not paying less per unit).
3. **Run parallel workstreams.** Sourcing events (formal RFPs for competitive categories), negotiation (direct renegotiation for concentrated categories, often using benchmark pricing data as leverage), and demand management (specification changes, consumption reduction, maverick-spend elimination) proceed in parallel rather than sequentially.
4. **Lock savings into the budget.** As with all cost transformation levers, procurement savings need to be reflected in the next budget cycle with an accountable owner, or they leak back within a year through supplier price creep.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Under-investing in spend cube quality.** A procurement program built on messy, inconsistent spend data produces unreliable savings estimates and undermines credibility with the client's procurement function from day one.
- **Treating every category as a sourcing-event candidate.** Running a competitive RFP on a concentrated, hard-to-switch category wastes time and can damage a supplier relationship the client actually needs to preserve.
- **Ignoring demand management.** Teams default to price negotiation and skip consumption reduction, which is frequently the larger and more durable saving — especially for indirect categories like travel, facilities, and professional services.

## Overview

Workforce planning models future headcount and skill requirements against the client's strategic plan, identifying gaps in advance rather than reacting to shortages or surpluses as they emerge. It connects a client's strategy and operating model work directly to a concrete talent implication — often the missing link between a strategy document and the people who have to execute it.

## When to Use It

Use as a companion to strategic planning or operating model engagements, wherever the strategic plan implies a material change in headcount, skill mix, or location footprint — a common but often-skipped step, since strategy work frequently stops short of the workforce implications.

## Framework

1. **Translate the strategic plan into workforce demand.** For each major strategic initiative (new market entry, digital transformation, cost program), estimate the resulting headcount and skill implications — growth initiatives typically require new capability the organization doesn't currently have; cost programs typically require headcount reduction concentrated in specific functions, not evenly spread.
2. **Build the supply-side view.** Project current workforce forward accounting for attrition, retirement, and internal mobility, by skill category and location — this is the "what we'll have if nothing changes" baseline against which demand is compared.
3. **Identify gaps by skill category, not just aggregate headcount.** An aggregate view can show workforce roughly in balance while masking a severe shortage in one critical skill category and a surplus in another — the gap analysis needs enough granularity to be actionable for hiring, reskilling, or redeployment decisions.
4. **Build a differentiated closing plan per gap type.** Shortage gaps close through hiring, reskilling existing talent, or redeployment from surplus areas — each with different cost, speed, and feasibility trade-offs; surplus gaps require a managed reduction plan, ideally through attrition and redeployment before resorting to involuntary reduction.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Aggregate-only headcount modeling.** Missing the skill-category granularity that makes the gap analysis actually useful for hiring and reskilling decisions.
- **Treating workforce planning as a one-time exercise disconnected from strategy refreshes.** Workforce plans go stale as fast as the strategic plans that drive them — refresh on the same cadence as the strategic planning cycle, not as a separate one-off exercise.
- **Underestimating reskilling timelines.** Assuming existing talent can be reskilled into new capability areas on an unrealistically fast timeline, rather than validating against how long comparable reskilling programs actually take.

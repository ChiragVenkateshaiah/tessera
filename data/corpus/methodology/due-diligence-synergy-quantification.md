## Overview

Synergy quantification estimates the cost and revenue benefits achievable by combining an acquirer and target — the incremental value that justifies paying a premium over the target's standalone worth. Overstated synergy estimates are one of the most common causes of value-destructive M&A, which makes rigor here disproportionately important relative to the time typically allocated to it in a deal process.

## When to Use It

During due diligence, in parallel with commercial and financial diligence, whenever the deal thesis depends materially on synergies (as opposed to a pure standalone-value acquisition). Also used post-signing to build the detailed integration synergy tracking plan.

## Framework

1. **Categorize synergies by type.** Cost synergies (procurement scale, SG&A/shared services consolidation, facility rationalization) are generally more certain and faster to realize than revenue synergies (cross-selling, combined go-to-market) — weight confidence and timing accordingly rather than treating all synergy categories as equally reliable.
2. **Build bottom-up, not top-down.** Synergy estimates built from a top-down percentage-of-combined-revenue assumption are not defensible. Build from specific, named initiatives (e.g., "consolidate two overlapping manufacturing facilities," "combine duplicate corporate functions") with individually sized savings.
3. **Net out one-time costs to achieve.** Every synergy has an implementation cost — severance, system integration, facility exit costs. Report net synergy value (synergy minus cost-to-achieve), not gross, and be explicit about the multi-year phasing of both.
4. **Assign a confidence-weighted case.** Present synergies in tiers (high-confidence, medium-confidence, stretch) rather than a single blended number — this is what lets a deal team make an informed judgment about how much synergy value to actually underwrite into the purchase price.

**Worked example.** An industrials manufacturer evaluating acquisition of a smaller regional competitor initially received a synergy estimate from the deal advisory team based on a standard "5% of combined revenue" rule of thumb — a top-down number with no bottom-up support. Rebuilding bottom-up identified two overlapping manufacturing facilities within reasonable geographic proximity, procurement scale benefits from combined raw-material purchasing, and consolidation of duplicate back-office functions as the primary, high-confidence cost synergies — a materially smaller but far more defensible number than the initial top-down estimate, with revenue synergies (cross-selling into each company's existing customer base) kept in a separate, lower-confidence stretch case given the execution risk involved.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Top-down synergy estimates.** Percentage-of-revenue rules of thumb are not defensible and tend to systematically overstate achievable synergies, particularly on the revenue side.
- **Ignoring cost-to-achieve.** Gross synergy figures without a corresponding one-time cost estimate overstate the deal's true economics and set unrealistic post-close expectations.
- **Overweighting revenue synergies.** Revenue synergies are the least certain and slowest to realize category — deals that lean heavily on them to justify the price paid carry materially more risk than the headline synergy number suggests.

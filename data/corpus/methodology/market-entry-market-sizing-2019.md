## Overview

The share-of-wallet method sizes a market in a single top-down pass: take a published category spend figure, apply a share-of-wallet factor for the client's target segment, then apply a capture rate to arrive at one obtainable-revenue number. It was Meridian's standard quick-sizing approach for market entry work from 2016 to 2019, chosen because it can be built in an afternoon from desk research alone.

## When to Use It

Used whenever a client asked "how big is this market" early in a market entry engagement and the team needed a headline figure for the first steering committee, before any customer-level data was available.

## Framework

1. **Start from the category spend figure.** Take the most recent analyst or trade-body estimate of total category spend in the target geography. This is treated as the total addressable market.
2. **Apply the share-of-wallet factor.** Multiply by the share of customer wallet the target segment devotes to the category, taken from the same analyst source or a comparable market. The result is the serviceable market.
3. **Apply the capture rate.** Multiply by a capture rate drawn from the firm's capture-rate table (5% for a new entrant, 10% for an adjacent-category player, 15% for an incumbent extending into a new geography). The result is the obtainable market.
4. **Report the obtainable market as a single figure.** Present one number to the client, rounded to the nearest million, with the analyst source cited.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Stale analyst figures.** Category spend estimates more than two years old should be inflated by the published category growth rate before use.
- **Mixing geographies.** Make sure the share-of-wallet factor and the category spend figure describe the same geography and year.

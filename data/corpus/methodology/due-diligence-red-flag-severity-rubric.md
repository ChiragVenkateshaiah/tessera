## Overview

The red flag reporting page covers how to write up findings. This page covers how to grade them. A diligence report where every finding is presented at the same weight forces the investment committee to do the triage the diligence team was engaged to do.

## When to Use It

Apply at the point a finding is first logged, not at report-writing time. Grading late produces a severity assessment shaped by the conclusion the team has already reached.

## Framework

**The three tiers.**

| Tier | Definition | Required response | Who decides |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deal-Breaker | Undermines the investment thesis, or creates an unquantifiable liability | Escalate within 24 hours; work stops on dependent workstreams | Deal principal |
| Price-Adjusting | Quantifiable value impact that changes the price, structure, or warranty package | Quantify, then carry into the negotiation position | Workstream lead, confirmed by deal principal |
| Monitor | Real but manageable; becomes a post-close integration or governance item | Log with a named post-close owner | Workstream lead |

**Indicators by tier.**

- **Deal-Breaker**
  - Thesis invalidation
    - Core market is structurally declining, not cyclically soft
      - *Escalation trigger:* two independent sources contradict management's growth narrative
    - Key customer relationship is ending or has materially changed terms
      - *Escalation trigger:* any customer above a material revenue share has served notice or is out to tender
    - The asset's differentiation rests on a capability that has already been replicated by competitors
  - Unquantifiable liability
    - Open regulatory investigation with an indeterminate outcome
      - *Escalation trigger:* counsel cannot bound the exposure
    - Environmental or site remediation obligation with no scoping study
    - Litigation where an adverse finding would exceed the equity cheque
  - Integrity
    - Evidence of deliberate misstatement in management-prepared information
      - *Escalation trigger:* any single instance — this tier has no materiality threshold
    - Access to information persistently obstructed without explanation

- **Price-Adjusting**
  - Earnings quality
    - Reported EBITDA relies on adjustments that do not survive scrutiny
      - *Escalation trigger:* adjustments exceed a threshold share of reported EBITDA
    - Revenue recognition policy change in the period that flatters the trend
    - Maintenance capex has been deferred, overstating sustainable earnings
  - Balance sheet
    - Working capital normalised level differs materially from the deal assumption
    - Debt-like items not captured in the net debt schedule
      - *Escalation trigger:* any newly identified item above the deal's materiality threshold
    - Inventory or receivables provisioning below what ageing supports
  - Commercial
    - Customer concentration above the level the thesis assumed
    - Contract terms materially worse than represented, including change-of-control triggers
    - Pricing achieved through discounting that is not sustainable at the assumed volume

- **Monitor**
  - Operational
    - Systems landscape more fragmented than represented
    - Key-person dependency without documented succession
    - Process controls weaker than the acquirer's standard, but not producing errors
  - Organisational
    - Culture or ways-of-working differences that will complicate integration
    - Compensation structures inconsistent with the acquirer's framework
  - Compliance hygiene
    - Policy documentation gaps with no evidence of underlying breach
    - Registration or filing administrative lapses, remediable pre- or post-close

**Grading discipline.** Two rules keep the rubric honest. First, tier on the finding as it stands, not on the mitigation you expect to negotiate — a Price-Adjusting finding does not become a Monitor because the team is confident of a warranty. Second, a finding can move up a tier as evidence accumulates but should not move down without the deal principal recording why.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Tier inflation late in the process.** A team under time pressure grades more findings as Monitor because Monitor requires no further work. Grade at logging, before the deadline pressure exists.
- **Aggregation blindness.** Several Price-Adjusting findings in the same area frequently constitute a Deal-Breaker in combination. Review the register as a whole at least once, explicitly looking for this.
- **Treating integrity findings as materiality questions.** They are not. A single deliberate misstatement changes how every other management representation should be weighted.
- **Logging without an owner.** A Monitor finding with no named post-close owner will not survive the transition from diligence to integration.

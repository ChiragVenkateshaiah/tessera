## Overview

This page holds the indicative peer ranges we use when starting an SG&A benchmarking exercise. It is a reference sheet, not a methodology page — read the SG&A benchmarking methodology first for how to select a peer set and decompose a gap. The numbers below are directional starting points for framing a conversation, never a target and never a substitute for building a client-specific peer set.

## When to Use It

Use these ranges in the first two weeks of a cost transformation diagnostic, when you need a rough sense of whether a client's overhead structure is plausibly high before the real peer analysis is built. Do not put these figures in a client deliverable without replacing them with a client-specific peer set.

## Framework

**Indicative overhead ratios by industry.** Ranges reflect broad, publicly reported bands rather than a specific peer group. Treat the spread within each row as more informative than the midpoint.

| Industry | SG&A as % revenue | Cost-to-income ratio | Technology spend as % revenue | Corporate-function FTE per $100M revenue | Primary comparability caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Retail banking | n/a — use cost-to-income | 45–65% | 7–11% | 25–45 | Branch density and wealth/insurance mix distort the ratio heavily |
| Grocery and mass retail | 18–26% | n/a | 1–3% | 8–18 | Own-brand penetration and store-format mix change the cost base shape |
| Industrials manufacturing | 12–20% | n/a | 2–4% | 12–25 | Vertical integration depth moves cost between COGS and SG&A |
| Specialty pharma | 30–45% | n/a | 4–7% | 30–55 | R&D treatment and commercial-stage vs pre-launch status dominate |
| Business services | 25–38% | n/a | 3–6% | 20–40 | Delivery labour may sit in COGS or SG&A depending on policy |
| Insurance | n/a — use expense ratio | 25–35% expense ratio | 5–9% | 20–35 | Distribution model (direct vs broker) drives most of the variance |

**Normalization adjustments.** Apply these to both the client and the peer set before comparing. A gap that survives all five is a real gap; one that doesn't is an accounting artefact.

| Adjustment | What to remove or restate | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| One-off items | Restructuring charges, litigation settlements, impairments | A single-year snapshot otherwise reflects events, not run-rate structure |
| Capitalization policy | Software and development costs capitalized vs expensed | Shifts cost between SG&A and depreciation, changing the ratio without changing spend |
| COGS/SG&A boundary | Delivery, service, and field labour classification | The most common source of a spurious gap in services businesses |
| Business mix | Revenue-weighted blend of segment-level ratios | A diversified client compared to a monoline peer produces a meaningless number |
| Lease accounting | Operating vs finance lease treatment | Moves occupancy cost out of SG&A and into depreciation and interest |

**How to use the ranges.** Position the client against the band, not the midpoint. A client at the top of a band is a candidate for further work; a client outside the band entirely is more often a comparability problem than a genuine outlier, and should trigger a re-check of the normalization adjustments above before any target-setting conversation.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Quoting these figures to a client.** They are internal framing numbers drawn from broad public reporting. A client's finance team will ask for the peer set behind any number in a deliverable, and "our internal reference sheet" is not an answer that survives that question.
- **Using the midpoint as a target.** The band width is the finding. A narrow band means the ratio is structurally determined and a gap is meaningful; a wide band means business-model variation dominates and the ratio alone tells you very little.
- **Skipping normalization because the numbers look close.** A client that appears at peer median before normalization may sit well above it afterwards, and finding that out late undermines the whole diagnostic.

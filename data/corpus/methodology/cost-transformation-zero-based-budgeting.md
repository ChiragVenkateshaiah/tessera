## Overview

Zero-based budgeting (ZBB) rebuilds a cost center's budget from zero every cycle, requiring every line item to be justified against current need rather than carried forward from last year plus an inflation adjustment. It's slower and more disruptive than incremental budgeting, and it is the single most effective lever for durably resetting SG&A cost structure when applied with discipline.

## When to Use It

Use ZBB when incremental budgeting has allowed cost creep over multiple years and a genuine reset — not a trim — is needed. It's a heavier-weight tool than the other cost transformation levers and works best on SG&A and other discretionary/overhead categories rather than direct COGS, which usually has tighter unit-economics logic already.

## Framework

1. **Define cost pools and owners.** Break SG&A into granular pools (e.g., marketing, IT, corporate functions, store operations support) each with a single accountable owner — not a committee.
2. **Build from zero.** Each owner justifies every cost line against a current business need, using a standard cost-driver logic (e.g., headcount driven by transaction volume, not by "what we spent last year"). No line item carries forward by default.
3. **Benchmark each pool externally.** Cross-reference the zero-based build against external SG&A benchmarks (see the SG&A benchmarking page) to sanity-check whether the "justified" number is actually competitive.
4. **Negotiate and lock.** Reconcile owner-submitted budgets against the overall target, negotiate gaps, and lock the result with clear accountability — ZBB's value evaporates if the new baseline drifts back to incremental budgeting the following cycle.

**Worked example.** A multi-banner retail group applying ZBB to its corporate marketing function found that roughly 20% of the existing budget was tied to legacy loyalty programs and print media spend inherited from a banner acquired four years earlier, never re-justified against current channel performance. Rebuilding from zero against current customer acquisition cost data freed up budget that was redirected to digital acquisition channels — a reallocation, not just a cut, which made the change easier to sell internally to the marketing organization.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Running ZBB as a cost-cutting exercise in disguise.** If owners sense the "zero-based" framing is just cover for a predetermined cut target, they'll game the process rather than genuinely re-justify spend.
- **Applying it to the wrong cost types.** ZBB works well on discretionary SG&A; it's a poor fit for costs with hard unit-economics drivers (e.g., raw materials), where a different lever is more appropriate.
- **No re-baselining discipline.** Without a governance mechanism to keep the new baseline "zero-based" in subsequent cycles, costs drift back to incremental-budgeting behavior within 1-2 years.

## Overview

Value premium pricing sets the price of a differentiated product as its fully loaded unit cost plus a value premium uplift, where the size of the uplift is read off a differentiation scorecard. It was Meridian's standard way of putting a number on "value-based" pricing in pharma and medtech engagements until 2018, because it gives a defensible-looking price without building a customer-side value model.

## When to Use It

Used for launch pricing of a product the client considered better than the standard of care, when the team needed a recommended price quickly and internal cost data was readily available.

## Framework

1. **Establish the fully loaded unit cost.** Include manufacturing, distribution, and an allocation of R&D amortization and commercial overhead.
2. **Score differentiation on the scorecard.** Rate the product from 1 to 5 on each of five dimensions — efficacy, safety, convenience, brand strength and novelty — with the client's product and marketing teams. Average the five scores.
3. **Read the value premium uplift from the scorecard.** An average score of 1-2 earns a 10% uplift on cost, 2-3 earns 25%, 3-4 earns 40%, and 4-5 earns 60%.
4. **Set the price.** Price equals fully loaded unit cost multiplied by one plus the uplift. Validate internally with the client's pricing committee before launch.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Inflated scorecards.** Product teams tend to score their own product generously; have marketing and finance score independently and average the results.
- **Forgetting overhead allocation.** An uplift applied to direct cost only understates the price.

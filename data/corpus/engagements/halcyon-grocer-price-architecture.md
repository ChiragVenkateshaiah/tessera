## Client Context

Project Halcyon was a price architecture review for a top-three UK grocer operating roughly 2,800 stores across convenience, supermarket and large-format banners. The client's own-label tiers had drifted: its value tier sat within 4% of the branded equivalent on over a third of key value items, eroding the tier's reason to exist.

## Scope

Rebuild the good/better/best price ladder across 1,150 key value items, set banner-level price zones, and quantify the margin at stake before the client's spring price investment announcement.

## Approach

The team estimated item-level elasticities from 104 weeks of loyalty-card transaction data, clustered stores into six price zones by competitive intensity, and modelled tier gaps against the client's hard-discounter exposure.

## Findings

- Re-opening the value-to-standard tier gap to 18-22% on the drifted items recovered an estimated £340m of annual gross margin at flat volume.
- Convenience stores were over-discounted relative to their competitive set; collapsing them into two zones from four removed £60m of unnecessary price investment.
- Hard-discounter overlap, not store format, was the strongest predictor of price sensitivity.

## Outcome

The client adopted the six-zone structure and the revised tier gaps in its spring price reset. The steering committee asked for a follow-on review of promotional depth.

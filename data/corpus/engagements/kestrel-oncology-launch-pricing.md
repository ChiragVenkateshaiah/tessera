## Client Context

Project Kestrel supported a mid-cap European biotech preparing the US and EU5 launch of its first approved therapy, a second-line treatment for metastatic bladder cancer with a 4.1-month overall survival benefit over standard of care.

## Scope

Recommend a launch list price corridor, a payer value dossier strategy, and an outcome-based contracting option for the three largest US pharmacy benefit managers.

## Approach

The team built a cost-of-care model from claims data on 23,000 second-line patients, interviewed 31 payer medical directors across the US and EU5, and benchmarked against six comparable oncology launches since 2019.

## Findings

- A US list price corridor of $14,200-$15,800 per month was defensible against the value model; above $16,000, payer interviews indicated step-edits behind the incumbent.
- Germany's AMNOG benefit assessment was the binding constraint in Europe: a "considerable" added-benefit rating was achievable only with the quality-of-life endpoint data, which the client had not planned to submit.
- An outcome-based rebate tied to 12-month treatment persistence was attractive to two of the three PBMs.

## Outcome

The client launched within the recommended corridor and re-scoped its German dossier to include the quality-of-life endpoint.

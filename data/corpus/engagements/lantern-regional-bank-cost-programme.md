## Client Context

Project Lantern was a cost transformation for a Midwestern US regional bank with $62bn in assets, 410 branches and a cost-income ratio of 68% against a peer median of 57%.

## Scope

Identify $300m of run-rate savings over three years without reducing frontline lending capacity, and design the governance to hold the savings.

## Approach

Zero-based budgeting across 14 corporate cost pools, a branch network rationalization model built on branch-level profitability, and an operations spans-and-layers review covering 9,800 FTE.

## Findings

- Corporate functions carried 1.9x the peer median of management layers; flattening from nine layers to six released $85m.
- 96 branches fell below breakeven with an overlapping branch within two miles; consolidating them released $110m with modelled deposit attrition under 3%.
- Third-party spend held $70m of savings, concentrated in IT contractors and market data subscriptions.

## Outcome

The bank committed to $310m of savings, with branch consolidation phased over 18 months to manage regulatory and community commitments.
